An Azure savings plan is a commitment-based discount where you commit to a fixed **dollar amount per hour** for one or three years, and get lower prices on eligible usage in return. It is the flexible sibling of reservations — and, importantly for container work, **Azure Container Apps is covered**.

**How the discount works.** Benefits apply automatically **each hour** to eligible usage within the plan's scope, starting with the usage that receives the **highest discount** first. The discounted cost is deducted from your hourly commitment. Once the commitment is fully used in that hour, remaining eligible usage bills at normal pay-as-you-go rates.

Two consequences:
- **Unused commitment for an hour expires and does not roll over.** Commit to your floor, not your average — an hour where you under-run is money gone.
- Because the highest-discount usage is matched first, the plan automatically optimizes application for you. You do not choose what it covers.

**Discount level depends on product and term, not amount.** A larger commitment does not buy a deeper rate — only the 1-year vs 3-year choice and the specific product do. So there is no volume incentive to over-commit.

**Two savings plans exist.**

**Savings plan for compute** covers infrastructure costs from:
- Azure Virtual Machines
- Azure App Service
- Azure Functions **premium** plan
- Azure Container Instances
- Azure Dedicated Host
- **Azure Container Apps**
- Azure Spring Apps for Enterprise

**Savings plan for databases** covers infrastructure and software IP costs for SQL Database (including Hyperscale and serverless), SQL Managed Instance, Database for PostgreSQL and MySQL, Cosmos DB, DocumentDB, Database Migration Service, and SQL Server hourly licenses on VMs or Arc.

**What compute savings plans do not cover:** **software, networking, or storage** charges — the same exclusions as reservations. Licensing can be addressed with Azure Hybrid Benefit.

Note the nuance that other services running on eligible compute infrastructure may also benefit indirectly, since the discount attaches to the underlying infrastructure meter.

**The critical asymmetry versus reservations: savings plans cannot be cancelled or refunded.** Reservations allow exchanges and refunds up to $50,000 in a rolling 12 months. Savings plans allow neither. This makes the commitment sizing decision genuinely irreversible and is the strongest argument for committing conservatively.

**Flexibility is the upside.** Savings plan benefits apply across eligible **services and regions** automatically. Migrate a workload from VMs to Container Apps, move region, or change VM family, and the plan keeps applying. A reservation would have needed an exchange for any of those.

**Eligibility and purchase.** Available to **EA, MCA, or MPA** customers — EA requires offer type MS-AZR-0017P or MS-AZR-0148P (DevTest). Not available on a plain individual pay-as-you-go subscription. You can make a new commitment or **trade in one or more eligible reservations** for a savings plan. Payment up front or monthly, with the same total either way. Pricing is in USD for MCA/MPA and local currency for EA, and always billed in local currency — so MCA/MPA customers in non-USD currencies see monthly amounts vary with exchange rates.

Prices under savings plans are **subject to change**, with changes effective the first of the month, and current rates are used when applying benefits regardless of when the plan was purchased.

**Choosing between them — the actual decision.**

Use a **reservation** when:
- usage is stable and predictable in a known SKU and region
- you want the deepest possible discount (up to 72%)
- you value the ability to exchange or refund
- the workload is unlikely to move or be re-platformed

Use a **savings plan** when:
- spend is steady but its *shape* changes — you are migrating, re-platforming, or scaling across regions
- you run a mix of eligible services (VMs **and** Container Apps **and** App Service)
- you want one instrument instead of managing many reservations
- you would rather accept a smaller discount than risk stranded reservations

**A common combined strategy:** reservations on the truly immovable core (the database VMs that will not change for three years), a savings plan on the more dynamic remainder (the container platform, the app services, the VMs that get resized).

**For Container Apps specifically.** The savings plan is the only commitment discount available — there is no ACA reservation. So the sequence is: architect for cost first (scale to zero, jobs over always-on, right-size allocation, pack Dedicated profiles), establish what your irreducible hourly baseline actually is, then commit to that number. Committing before optimizing locks in waste you cannot refund.

**Determining the commitment.** Recommendations are available in **Azure Advisor**, the portal purchase experience, and the Benefit Recommendation APIs, computed from your eligible on-demand usage, your effective pay-as-you-go rates, and the 1- and 3-year savings plan rates.

**Interview framing.** "A savings plan is a dollars-per-hour commitment for one or three years that applies automatically across eligible compute services and regions — including Container Apps, which has no reservation option. The trade versus a reservation is a shallower discount for much more flexibility. Two things that drive the decision: unused hourly commitment expires and doesn't roll over, and savings plans can't be cancelled or refunded at all, unlike reservations which allow exchanges and refunds up to $50k a year. So you commit to your confident floor, and you do the architectural optimization before you commit, not after."
